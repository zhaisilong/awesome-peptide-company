# Source review: initial cases

Review date: 2026-11-17. The nine files in `tests/fixtures/` reproduce the implementation plan's examples and are not a statement that every example has been verified. The production `data/` files are a separate conservative selection. Sources establish only the facts they actually state; a reported relationship does not establish a legal transaction date or an asset transfer.

1. **Reuters and the inaccessible WOWTALE page.** The WOWTALE URL returned HTTP 403. The full [Reuters report, republished by StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/ByteDance%2Bcompletes%2B%24290%2Bmillion%2Bfundraising%2Bfor%2BAI%2Bdrug%2Bunit%2Bafter%2Bits%2Bspin-off%2C%2Bsources%2Bsay/27066461.html), was accessible. It attributes the funding and ownership figures to unnamed sources and says ByteDance and investors did not immediately comment. The Reuters report and [VCBeat via 36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3986744042765062) both describe a spin-off, but the latter cites Reuters elsewhere in the article. Without establishing independent sourcing, production conservatively assigns the VCBeat article and Reuters the same `origin_key`. The spin-off and figures remain `reported`. The report date is used where the spin-off date was not established. The 2021 date in the fixture refers to the ByteDance team, not the incorporation of Anew Labs, and is not used as its founding date.
2. **Syneron Series B original.** The [company's PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/syneron-bio-announces-completion-of-150-million-series-b-financing-302733591.html) is available, dated April 3, 2026, and says the round closed March 31. Production cites it directly. It describes an international life-science fund as lead, with Decheng Capital and CDH VGC as co-leads.
3. **SCMP metadata.** The accessible [SCMP article header](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3352032/bytedances-drug-unit-presents-ai-designed-therapies-global-conferences) gives the date May 2, 2026, the title and the alternate names Anew Therapeutics and ByteDance AI Drug Discovery. The protected remainder was not accessed. Metadata in production reflects the visible header, not the original fixture's undated placeholder.
4. **Anew official website.** [Anew Labs](https://anewbt.com/) has a public site. Its [AnewDPSA research page](https://anewbt.com/research/dpsa/) describes work on macrocyclic-peptide permeability, providing direct evidence for peptide relevance. The site’s HTML title explicitly pairs “Anew Labs” with “新生实验室,” supporting the Chinese alias. The site lists Shanghai as an office; [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/ByteDance%2Bcompletes%2B%24290%2Bmillion%2Bfundraising%2Bfor%2BAI%2Bdrug%2Bunit%2Bafter%2Bits%2Bspin-off%2C%2Bsources%2Bsay/27066461.html) explicitly describes Anew Labs as headquartered in Shanghai, supporting the city field. The site itself is not treated as confirmation of a particular spin-off date, financing terms or Protenix ownership.
5. **Protenix ownership and maintenance.** The [official Protenix repository](https://github.com/bytedance/Protenix) remains under ByteDance, and its original citation names ByteDance AML AI4Science Team. [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3846956646124036) reports a transfer of the drug unit's platform and describes earlier team integration, but does not explicitly establish a transfer of Protenix maintenance. Production therefore does not assert Anew Labs as its maintainer. This is an observation made during research, not a network check on every build.
6. **Syneron headquarters and founding date.** The [partnership release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/syneron-bio-announces-strategic-collaboration-with-astrazeneca) describes a Beijing research centre; the place in a press-release dateline does not establish headquarters. No authoritative incorporation record was verified during this review. Production leaves headquarters and founding date unset.
7. **Syneron dry/wet loop.** An [AACR conference proceedings search result](https://www.aacr.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/AACR2025_Proceedings_050725.pdf) identifies abstract 4384 as a Syneron-associated peptide research lead. The PDF could not be retrieved in full through the browser because of its size, and a [patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025162428A1/en) alone does not establish an AI-driven closed-loop process or the operational details needed for L3. The release describes an intelligent high-throughput platform. No loop level is assigned in production pending direct evidence satisfying the assessment rubric.
8. **AstraZeneca Molecular AI reorganisation.** No first-party evidence was found establishing the relationship between the named innovation unit and Molecular AI, or a reorganisation of the latter. No such event is recorded. The [Molecular AI organisation](https://github.com/MolecularAI) and its [PepINVENT repository](https://github.com/MolecularAI/PepINVENT) establish the team context and peptide work.
9. **Seed AI4S management changes.** A [May 29 report by The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33265218) discusses an adjustment and quotes a person close to ByteDance denying a split plan at that point. It does not establish that the later Anew transaction merged the particular Protenix team into Anew Labs. No assertion based solely on the encyclopedia lead is included, and the fixture's `merged_into` event is not promoted to production.
10. **awesome-peptide DOI matching.** The sister repository's `data/paper.csv` contains DOI `10.1039/d4sc07642g` (PepINVENT), but that table has no stable entry-ID column. No stable `awepep_id` is invented. The other three fixture DOIs were not found in its local `data/` directory. DOI links remain usable and IDs stay empty.

The [peptide benchmark preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.09.642277v2.full) was available through an indexed full-text search result, while direct fetching returned HTTP 403. Its direct-page `accessed` date has therefore been cleared. The separately opened [official bioRxiv API record](https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2025.03.09.642277) supplies the abstract, supporting the narrow claim that Protenix was evaluated on protein–peptide complexes. The two records share an origin and are not independent evidence. The API identifies the linked second version as 15 March 2025; 13 March is the first version's date. No paper, patent or press release is copied into the dataset.
